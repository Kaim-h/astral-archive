The study of physical storage

Objects should belong to their furniture.

Fewer arbitrary positions, more explicit geometry. The diagrams use the Archive's three actual palettes and the book and shelf proportions established in the repository.

Prototype III · symbolic 2D

01 · The book shelf

Existing 42-unit spines with their ribbon bookmarks; every bottom meets the same actual shelf surface.

Source-informed reinterpretation of Book.qml and Shelf.qml, not a runtime QML renderer.

02 · The scroll rack

One continuous partitioned rack. Lines and their intersections define each square, diamond, or triangular cell; hexes form a separate tessellation.

Calculating geometry…

Five scroll radius tiers. Gravity-biased, collision-tested packing freezes after each edit. Overflow is reported, never hidden inside walls.

03 · The upright holder

A compact plate-holder structure with actual bottom grooves and edge-on objects, not a row of overlapping front-facing icons.

All items are seated in defined slots and retained by the holder's side rails. Increase face exposure to explore a more angled drawing.

04 · The exhibition shelf

Illustrated artworks hang against the backing. Sculptures, instruments, and parcels are anchored by their physical bases.

All images are symbolic drawings; they do not preview the contents of actual files. Hover a drawn object for its metadata description.
Scope: This is a browser source prototype with synthetic objects. Its geometry is calculated rather than hand-positioned, but it is not a full rigid-body dynamics engine. It does not modify your files, repository, or Quickshell installation. The next technical step is to translate the validated placement rules into QML policy and reuse the existing shelf widget directly.
